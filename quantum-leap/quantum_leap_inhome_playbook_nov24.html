TL;DR

  • Do what you told them you'd do. Gift up front, test the water first. No survey, no company talk first.
  • "The battle is won at the sink. It's also lost at the sink." Every sink demo, every home. Close out each one: "Would you ever need to buy bleach again?" "If she says it, it's true. If you tell her, it's debatable."
  • "People don't buy things that save them money. People buy things they want." Pitch benefits and waste, never "save."
  • Get the cleaning product and bottled water commitment right after the hydrologic page, before any sink demo. "If you're getting it during the sink demo, you're losing. If you get it during the close, you've lost."
  • Close: 3 questions, then the T close on a yellow legal pad. "When I came in your house you had a bill. When I leave you're gonna have a bill." Our financing: $0 down, 0% interest, as low as $69/mo.
  • Pick one package. "Never lower the price. Raise the value." "Put on a $20,000 presentation to sell a $10,000 system."
  • Post close every sale to prevent the family discussion and cancellations.

Step-by-step appointment flow

ABefore the visit: don't confirm, just go

  • "If you confirm appointments, you're gonna lose more than half of them." Texting "is it still okay?" gives them an out: "people are brave on a phone… they're brave in a car."
  • "You wouldn't do that on an install. Why would you do it on an appointment?… Don't confirm the appointment. Just go."
  • Nobody home? Go next door and the three doors across the street:
"Hi Mr. Jones. I got an appointment with Mr. Smith right next door. He must be running late… I'm in the water treatment business. If there's a problem with your water, I'm gonna leave you information on our products and I hope you call me first… Right now we got a program going where about 90% of the people are gonna get this system for free." "How are you gonna do that?" "That's one of the things I'll show you when I come test your water. I promise you it's for real."

CFirst 10 minutes: gift, hardness test, precip test

  • Give the gift as soon as you walk in. "We lose a 10, $12,000 sale over a 40, 50, a hundred dollar gift card. It's ridiculous."
  • No survey up front: you'll hear "What does this got to do with testing my water? I didn't know you were here to sell me something."
  • Hardness test on tap and treated water with a stand-alone hardness classification table (US Public Health Service). Count the drops out loud.
"Tom and Mary, according to them, treated water is anywhere from zero to one grains per gallon. The worst on this chart is over 10 and a half… 15 drops… your water is so bad it's off the chart." (At 7 or 8 grains: "Three or four more grains, you're off the chart.")
  • Mini demonstrator: "This is a miniature demonstrator. I'm going to get into this more later."
  • If they want the price now: "Mr. Jones, 90% of our people qualify to get the system for free. I think you're going to qualify, but I have to finish the rest of the test, and I need both you and your wife here… Sit down. Pay attention. I'm sure you're going to like what I got to see."
  • Precip test: "This is your water treated. This is your water not treated."
"Tom and Mary, look at this already. Look at that stuff. This test isn't even done yet. Look at the difference. Can you see why your 12 grains, 15 grains… is so bad it's off the chart? Look at this. You wouldn't want to drink this stuff much longer."
"If you were gonna get a glass of water, you want to drink this one or this one? Naturally I'm sure you're gonna pick this one."
"You ever look at your shower doors, your bathroom tile… white streaks and spots? That's this stuff right here. You ever boil water in a pot? … This is why they tell you to run vinegar through a drip coffee maker."
If asked what's in the tube: "It's a mixture of inorganic minerals, plus anything else can end up in your water supplies. But here's what I know. I gotta be better off with this clear one than this one right here. If this was sugar, it's too much. Whatever it is, I don't want it. I'm better off drinking this one."
  • Well water: do iron and other tests here. City water: chlorine test here if you want. "Do all your water tests right up front."
  • Carry every test to the table: they're "little sales reps saying, look at me, look at me."
  • Transition: "This test is going to take 15, 20 minutes to settle out. While it's settling out, let me tell you about who we are."

DCompany credibility, then the hydrologic cycle

  • "You have to hit the credibility real hard here and not just glaze over it." Years in business, the brand, big commercial accounts: "Home Depot could have bought and set up with anybody. They picked us. If it's good enough for them, it should be good enough for you."
  • Hydrologic page: chlorine, chemicals, pharmaceuticals, PFAS, microplastics, pesticides, insecticides, "everything has ended up in the drinking water." "Most of all the drinking water in America comes from the same place they're dumping all the junk."
"Is there anything on this page you would want to leave in your water and drink?" ("None of it.") "This is why water softeners are obsolete. They can't take this stuff out… that's what's gonna make you sick eventually. Not the hard water."
  • After the hardness test, "hard water and soft water leaves my vocabulary." Say tap water, bad water, treated water, polluted water.

ECleaning product + bottled water commitment (before the sink demo)

"Tom and Mary, let's talk about cleaning products. Have you seen the price of this stuff today, along with everything else at the grocery store? If I gave you $35 and I said go to the grocery store and buy all the bar soaps, laundry soap, dish soap, shampoos, bath oils, body creams, hand lotions, conditioners, Ajax, Lime-A-Way, everything you need to clean your home for the average family of four, am I really gonna get change back at today's prices?" ("No.") "Most families gotta add another five or 10. What do you think you are?"
"Let's talk about bottled water. Most families today are using anywhere from five to 10 bottles a day. How many you think you're using?" (10 a day × 30 = 300 bottles × 50 cents = $150 — "let's low ball it down to 50 bucks a month.")
  • "I write that $40 a week down… and I don't say another word about it."
  • Where Robbie's $35 comes from: the soap companies' brochures. "Five-year value is around $9,500… divide by 60 months, it's $158 and change. There's roughly my $40 a week."
  • No sheet or survey. "Nobody wants to be a statistic." Say "cleaning products," not "soap products."

Urgency and the cost to wait

  • "No urgency in your presentation, you're gonna have lower demos and lower closing rates." You need "passion, enthusiasm, and urgency."
  • "If we don't make a big deal about this water, they're not gonna make a big deal about the water." "We gotta be animators, we gotta be entertainers."
  • Every demo: "How long do you want to continue washing in this stuff? How long do you want to give your kids a bath in this stuff? How long do you want your kids drinking this stuff?… Which one would you rather have? When would you want to have it?"
  • "Mr. Jones, why would you wanna wait? You've got a mess here. Your water's not that good. You're already paying for it."
  • Cost to wait: "$72 a month… times six months, it's almost 500 bucks extra you'd have had in your pocket, and you'd still have the clean water."
  • "If you were gonna stop the bleeding and do something about your water and you're already paying for it, when would you do it?" ("Right away.") Start writing.
  • The waste side "goes up every week, every month, every year… a runaway train." Ours is fixed.

Good / Better / Best

  • "Good, better, and best, or as I call it, weaker, weak, and strong." "If you're skilled and trained properly, you should never, ever have to drop."
