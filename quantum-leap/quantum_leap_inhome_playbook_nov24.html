TL;DR

  • Do what you promised first. We booked a water test, so test the water first. No survey, no company story, no price talk until the tests are on the table.
  • The sale is made at the sink, not at the close. Do every sink demo in every home. End each one with a question the customer answers: "Would you still need to buy fabric softener?" When they say no, it's true.
  • Sell clean water and waste, not "savings." Robbie says nobody tells you on the way out that they bought to save money. They buy the hand wash, the glasses, the shower.
  • Get the money numbers early. Ask about bottled water and cleaning products right after the "why water is different" page, before the sink demo. Ask late and people lowball you.
  • Close with 3 questions, then a T close on paper. Use their own numbers next to our payment of as low as $69/mo ($0 down, 0% interest).
  • Recommend one system and never lower the price. Dropping the price kills trust. Raise the value instead.
  • Post close every sale. Ask each spouse, in front of the other, why they decided. It stops the "did you really want this?" talk in the car that turns into a cancel.

⚠️ Biscayne Water rules (these beat anything Robbie says)

  • A precip or hardness test shows hardness minerals (mostly calcium and magnesium). Never say it shows pollution, or that the water is unsafe, dirty, or "poison." (Florida Statute 817.558.)
  • Warranty is an extended 15 year warranty. Never say "lifetime."
  • Financing line: "$0 down, 0% interest, as low as $69/mo." Don't make up other terms. Custom pricing needs a manager.
  • Miami-Dade and Broward city water uses chloramine (chlorine plus ammonia). A basic free chlorine test can miss it. Test total chlorine.
  • Never mention BizHunt. No fake deadlines or "only today" deals.
Robbie lines we do NOT use: "90% of people get it for free" · "you wouldn't want to drink this much longer" · "if this was sugar it's too much" · "how polluted / corrosive your water is" · "I can't afford to lose my job" · chlorine "kills the good bacteria in you" · dishwasher steam is "chloroform" · "appliances wear out 50% faster." These are health or safety claims we can't prove, or pitches that bend the truth.

Step-by-step appointment flow

ABefore the visit: don't give them an easy out

  • Robbie's view: a yes/no confirm call or text ("is it still OK?") loses more than half of appointments, and few ever get rebooked. People say no by text far more easily than face to face.
  • Our version: don't ask "still OK?". Send a heads-up instead: "Hi Maria, it's Luis from Biscayne Water. I'm on my way for your water test at 4:00."
  • If nobody's home, knock on the neighbors' doors while you're there and offer the same free water test.

Source: Should You Be Confirming Your Appointments? · Where's The Urgency

BAt the door

If they say "we changed our minds" or "not a good time," don't just leave. Ask once, politely:

"I understand. I'm already here, and testing water is what I do. Give me 10 minutes for two quick tests. If there's an issue, I'll leave you the info, and if you ever decide to do something about it, I hope you call me first."
"What are you selling?" → "I'm in water treatment. First I test your water. What it costs depends on your water, so I can't say until I test it."
  • Only one spouse home? Do a short "spike": the hardness and precip tests and the soap flask. Then rebook on the spot for when both can be there: "She'll have questions about laundry and dishes that I should answer for her."
  • Robbie: the person who gives you the hardest time at the door is often the easiest to sell. The one who says "come in, we're shopping around" is the hardest. Bring your A game.

Source: How to get in the home · Robbie's 7 Steps · Where's The Urgency

CFirst 10 minutes: gift first, then test the water

  • If a gift was promised to book the visit, hand it over as soon as you walk in. Don't mail it later or hold it until the end. If you break that promise, they won't trust anything else you say.
  • No survey first. Starting with questions gets you "What does this have to do with testing my water?"
  • Hardness test, on both waters. Run their tap water and water from your portable demonstrator side by side. Treated turns blue right away. Count the drops on their tap water out loud.
  • Use a stand-alone hardness chart (grains per gallon: 0–1 treated, 1–3.5 slightly hard, 3.5–7 moderately hard, 7–10.5 hard, 10.5+ very hard). A separate chart reads as more credible than a page from our own pitch book.
"You're at 12 grains. The top of this chart is 10.5. Your water is so hard it's off the chart." (At 8: "Three more grains and you're off the chart.")
  • Precip test, both waters. Call them "your water treated" and "your water not treated." Both are their water.
  • Don't explain the demonstrator yet. Just say: "This is a mini version of our system. I'll get into it later." Talking about resin and carbon now gets you "What does it cost?" too early.
  • Tie it to their home:
"Those white spots on your shower doors, the white crust in your pots, the spots on your car, the vinegar you run through the coffee maker? That's this right here. Those are hardness minerals."
If asked "what is that?": "Those are hardness minerals, mostly calcium and magnesium. They're what leaves the scale and spots in your home." Do NOT say it's dirt, pollution, or unsafe to drink.
  • City water: run a total chlorine (chloramine) test right here too. Well homes: iron and sulfur tests here.
  • Bring every test to the table and line them up where they can see them. Robbie calls them "little sales reps" that keep selling while you talk.
  • Transition: "This takes about 15 minutes to settle. While it does, let me tell you who we are."

Source: Hardness and Precipitation Test · Updated Sink Demo For Modern Times! · Breaking Down The Sales Presentation · Where's The Urgency

DCompany, then "why water is different today"

  • Don't rush the company part. Share years in business, licenses, reviews, local installs, and the extended 15 year warranty. If they don't hear it, they'll say "we need to shop around" at the end.
  • "Water today" page (Robbie's hydrologic cycle): explain that city water is treated by adding things (here, chloramine), and that a softener only handles hardness. A whole-home system with carbon plus an RO at the kitchen sink does more.
  • After this page, stop saying "hard water" and "soft water." Say "tap water" and "treated water." Otherwise we sound like the $3,000 softener guy, and that's how cancels start: a coworker asks, "You paid what for a softener?"
"This is why a plain water softener isn't enough anymore. It's built for hardness. What we're talking about is a whole-home treatment system."

EGet the spending numbers BEFORE the sink demo

This is the most important setup in Robbie's system. Ask during the sink demo and they start to defend. Ask during the close and they lowball you.

"Let's talk about cleaning products. Have you seen the price of this stuff lately, along with everything else at the grocery store? If I gave you $35 to buy all the soap, laundry and dish detergent, shampoo, lotion, bleach and cleaners for a family of four for a week, would I really get change back at today's prices? Most families add $5 or $10. Where do you think you are?"
"Most families buy bottled water now. About how many bottles or cases a week?"
  • Write the numbers down and move on. Don't talk about them yet.
  • Say "cleaning products," not "soap." Most of what people buy is detergent and chemicals, and "soap" gets you a lowball answer.
  • No checklist form. Robbie: people "don't want to be a statistic." A preprinted chart gets you "that's not us."

Source: Robbie's Closing Sequence · Updated T Close and Objections · Value Building Demo = Higher Quality Leads · Hidden Objections

FThe sink demo: do every test and close out each one

Ask for: their bar of soap, a recently washed face cloth, and a bowl. Use their soap. If there's a big difference, it has to be the water. When you add soap, just say "this is soap." Talking up the brand makes them think about the soap instead of the water.

  • You never know which test is their hot button, so skip nothing. When a rep hits a slump, Robbie says it's usually because he started leaving tests out.

GEquipment: one system, kept simple

  • Nobody had a salesperson open up the back of their fridge to show the ice maker. They want to know three things: what it looks like, where it goes, and what it does for them. The sink demo already showed that last part.
  • Recommend one package as the expert: the whole-home system plus RO at the kitchen sink. Showing 5 packages and asking "which one?" is like a waiter listing every dessert. Robbie says that alone can cut your close rate by 25–30%.
"Here's what we recommend for your water. Before water goes anywhere in your home, it goes through this. Then we go one step further with an RO under your kitchen sink for cooking and drinking, with its own faucet. It comes with an extended 15 year warranty."

HClosing sequence

#Say itWhy
1"Is there any reason you wouldn't want clean water in your home today?" Then stop talking.Grades your demo. Expect "no, just how much is it?" If they say "we've drunk it for 30 years," the demo missed.
2"Is cost the only reason?"Pins down the one real objection.

  • If #1 is "no reason at all," go straight to the order blank close: "Can I get the correct spelling of your last name?" and start writing.

The T close, our version (handwritten, with their numbers)

Draw a big T on a yellow legal pad with a bold pen, right in front of them. Use no preprinted sheet. It has to be their numbers. Don't pause after saying their number back to them, because a pause invites them to change it.

  • Say the system's value, not its "cost" or "price." Robbie calls cost, price, payment, finance and contract "bad words." It's not a $10,000 decision. It's a $105-vs-$69 decision.
  • Only use bottled water and cleaning products. Adding water heater, coffee and appliance math confuses people, and confused people "think about it."
  • Use honest numbers. Robbie claims a 90% cut on all cleaning products and adds a "soap program." We don't have a soap program, so only count items the customer agreed they'd stop buying, plus bottled water.
  • Utility close for "we don't make snap decisions": "If your cable company called and said, 'We've been overcharging you, it should be less, and you get more channels,' would you need to think about it?"

IPost close: lock the sale on your way out

"Maria, why did you decide to have clean water in your home today?" (Let her answer while he listens.) "Carlos, why did you decide?" (Let him answer while she listens.) "Thank you. You're going to love it."
  • Each spouse hears the other say why they want it, so the "did you really want this?" talk never starts in the car.
  • Listen to the reasons. They tell you which demos sell. Robbie says almost nobody ever answers "to save money."
  • Robbie on cancels: people cancel when they don't know what they bought. If they can tell a coworker "it's not a softener, it's a whole-home treatment system," they'll defend it. He says dealers with permit delays of a week or more barely get cancels when the presentation is strong.

Objection handling

Robbie's rule: when you hear an objection tells you what broke. Before you've even mentioned price, it's a demo problem and no close will fix it. After the T close, it usually means they didn't understand. An objection is different from a condition, which is a real reason they can't buy right now (just lost a job, bankruptcy, in the middle of a refinance). If you can't close, book the next step with a date. Never leave it at "call me later."

Urgency and the cost of waiting (honest version)

  • Urgency starts at the door and runs through every test. Robbie: "If you don't make a big deal about their water, they won't." Be excited and energetic, not fake.
  • After each test: "Which would you rather have? When would you want it?"
  • Cost to wait, with their numbers: "Over the last 10 years, at about $105 a month, that's around $12,600 on bottled water and cleaners, and you still don't have clean water. Every month you wait is another $105."
  • Stop the bleeding: "If you're already paying for it and you were going to stop the bleeding, when would you do it?"
  • Not allowed: made-up deadlines, "price goes up tomorrow" (unless it's true and approved), or health and safety scares.

Good / Better / Best: Robbie's take

  • Robbie calls it "weaker, weak, strong." Leading with a menu, or stepping down to a cheaper package, trains reps to sell the easiest thing and drops close rates.
  • Recommend the best fit first. If a lower package is ever shown, it's the rep's call based on the water, not a price-cut tactic. Never drop the price on the same system. Robbie: "If he can drop it that fast, I was getting ripped off."
  • Ask this: "Do you want me to do as much as possible for your water, or get by with as little as I can?"
  • Robbie: "If you want to sell a $12,000 system, put on a $20,000 presentation." When the price ends up lower than they expected, they feel it's a good deal.
