We need to ask for the sale.

Is voicemail counted as a pickup? No. A pickup means a live person talking on the recording. When Viktor checked the short "answers" again, 16% turned out to be voicemail or a call screener, and he took them out.

Why did speed look bad? The team is fast in the daytime. The slow number came from overnight leads, plus one stat that measured time until someone answered, which isn't the same as time to the first dial.

Link 5: the day after

36% never got another dial after a call that didn't end in a sale. Only 1 of 86 sold later. If we don't call back, we don't sell.
The prize: going from about 10% kept sales to 14–16% on today's leads and today's ad spend models to a 20% net margin. We don't need a single extra lead to get there.
Note: reps ask more when the buyer already sounds ready, so 48% vs 16% overstates the lift. "99 of 128" does not depend on that.
What we must do

Seven fixes, in order

  1. Freeze the script for 14 days. One standard for every call: 3 asks, 2 dates, 1 next step. Stop changing the front of the call.
  2. 30 minutes of exit practice every morning. Practice the four exits: "spouse," "send me the quote," "not now," and "let me think."
  3. Review 5 calls a day, the same day. Look for calls where we gave a price and never asked for the sale.
  4. Open-decision list at 5 PM. Every qualified call that didn't sell gets a clock time and a second call within 48 hours.
  5. Add a "bad time" line to the opening. Turn "bad time" into a booked time before they hang up.
  6. Day-1 leads first, then dial every unreached lead daily through day 7. Call Spanish leads within 5 minutes.
  7. Log every WhatsApp and cell call in Close. 24% of kept sales have no recorded call, so we can't coach what we can't see.
