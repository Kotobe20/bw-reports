0. Setter Checklist

Before booking, confirm:

  • Homeowner
  • South Florida service area
  • City water or well water
  • Single-family home
  • Main water concern
  • Timeline
  • Number of bathrooms
  • Both decision-makers attending
  • Appointment within 24 to 72 hours
  • Lex’s Calendly slot confirmed
  • Confirmation sent
  • Night-before and morning-of confirmation completed

Call new leads within 60 seconds whenever possible. Speed to contact and speed to appointment directly improve show rates.

Full Word-for-Word Script

1. Open

“Hey [Name], it’s [Your Name] with Biscayne Water.

You filled out that form about your home’s water.

Did I catch you in the middle of something, or do you have 30 seconds?”

If busy

“No problem, is later today or tomorrow better for a quick call?”
Book the callback. Do not say, “I’ll try you later.”

If they have a second

“Perfect. I’ll be brief.”

2. Find Their Problem in Their Words

“Cool. What’s going on with your water that made you reach out?”

Listen. Do not interrupt.

Ask one follow-up:

“How long has that been bugging you?”

Or:

“What’s the most annoying part of that for you?”

Then repeat their words:

“So if I’m hearing you right, the big thing is [their words: spots, smell, kids’ skin, jugs]. That about right?”

Wait for confirmation.

Then deepen slightly:

“And if you got that handled, what would that change for you day to day?”
Do not diagnose over the phone. Do not create fear you cannot substantiate. You are identifying the reason for the visit.

3. Local Relevance and Proof

“That makes sense. We work with homeowners throughout Miami-Dade and Broward because the water concerns can vary significantly by city and by home.”

If relevant to their city:

“For example, our local water data has identified concerns involving arsenic and PFAS in parts of South Florida. We’ll verify what is actually happening at your home instead of guessing from a generic report.”
Use the 125x and 97x claims only when the underlying source and wording have been approved by management. Never tell a homeowner their water will cause a specific illness.

Then:

“We’ve completed 131 installs since August 22, and we’re currently at 5.0 stars on Google. The reason we still test the water at the home is that the right recommendation depends on the actual water, plumbing, and installation conditions.”

4. Set the Frame: What the In-Home Is For

“Got it. You called the right people.

Here’s how this works so we don’t waste your time:
- We send a water specialist out,
- he tests your actual tap water and checks where the system would go,
- shows you exactly what’s going on in your house,
- and then lays out the options and price.

If it makes sense, you can put an install date on the calendar right then. If it doesn’t, you at least know what you’re dealing with. Fair?”

Wait for “yeah,” “okay,” or equivalent.

Then add the Lex pre-frame:

“You’re also going to be in luck because Lex will be the person coming out. Lex is our [LEX TITLE], and he was hired from Culligan after training technicians throughout South Florida.”
“His job is to test the water, show you the results, lay out the options, and take payment at the visit if you decide the solution makes sense.”
“This is not a quote that gets emailed later for you to chase down. He does the evaluation, builds the recommendation, reviews it with you, and helps you decide while he’s there.”

5. Qualify the Home

“Perfect, a couple quick ones so I don’t send the wrong person:
- You own the home, right?
- City water or a well?
- How many full bathrooms?
- Anyone else living there full-time?”

If renter

“Got it. Since the system is installed into the home’s plumbing, we normally need the property owner involved. I’m going to route you to our renter/property-owner path so we don’t waste your time or schedule the wrong appointment.”
Tag: Renter Path. Do not book Lex unless the owner will attend.

If well water

“Thanks for letting me know. Lex’s appointment is designed primarily for city-water homes. I’m going to route you to our well-water path so the right specialist and testing process handle it.”
Tag: Well-Water Path. Do not book standard Lex appointment.

Decision-maker question

“Who else besides you would want a say in doing something about the water? Is [spouse/partner] usually part of decisions like this?”

If yes:

“Cool, I want them there. It’s way easier to answer questions once instead of you playing telephone later.”

Then lock it in:

“Can we count on both of you being there for the appointment?”

Wait for a clear yes.

If spouse cannot attend:

“No problem. Let’s find a time when both of you can be there. Lex builds the recommendation around the home, and it’s important that everyone who needs to decide hears the same information at the same time.”

6. Sell the Visit, Not the System

“And just so you know, this isn’t a 3-hour timeshare thing.

Lex’s job is simple: test your water, show you what he finds, and give you the real options and price.

If you love it, you can book a date. If not, no hard feelings – at least you know.”

Then reinforce the decision frame:

“The only thing we ask is that you’re prepared to make a decision at the appointment if the recommendation solves the problem you called about. Is that fair?”

Wait for agreement.

7. Book the Appointment

Open Lex’s Calendly, not HCP.

“Soonest I can get him there is [Day] at [Time] or [Day] at [Time]. Which works better for you and [spouse]?”

Offer same day first. Then next day. Then the day after.

If vague

“Which day is lighter for you, today, tomorrow, or the next day?”

Then give two exact times.

“Cool, I’ve got you for [repeat day/time].”

Confirm again:

“That’s [Day], [Date], at [Time], with you and [spouse] present. Correct?”

8. Create Commitment

“Two quick things before I let you go:
- He’ll need to get to your main shutoff and under the kitchen sink,
- If you can, have a recent water bill handy and think about roughly what you spend on bottled water and cleaners each month. That helps him size everything right.”

Then:

“Also, please make sure both decision-makers are home and that Lex has clear access to the areas we mentioned. That keeps the visit useful and prevents him from having to come back just to finish the evaluation.”

9. Financing Line

If they ask about payment:

“[MONEY LINE]”
Do not quote system pricing on the phone.

If they press:

“The reason we don’t quote a system before seeing the home is that the recommendation depends on the water results, plumbing layout, and installation conditions. Lex will show you the exact options and price at the visit.”

10. Confirm and Text

“What’s the best cell for a confirmation text?”

Confirm spelling of name, address, email, and spouse’s name.

“I’m texting you the appointment details from this number right now.

If anything changes, just text me here – otherwise, expect a quick ‘on the way’ text from Lex before he heads over.”

Then verify:

“I just sent it. Did you get it?”

Use the hot handoff:

“I’m also going to introduce you to Lex by text so you know exactly who is coming to your home. He’s the specialist I mentioned, and he’ll be prepared for your appointment.”

Objection Handlers

1. “We’re shopping around.”

“That makes sense. Most homeowners compare options before making a decision. The mistake is comparing prices before comparing what each system actually removes, what the installation includes, and whether it fits the home.”
“That’s exactly why we don’t quote blindly over the phone. Lex will test your water, inspect the installation area, and show you the options side by side.”
“Let’s get you the information you need to compare properly. I have [Day/Time] or [Day/Time]. Which works better for you and [spouse]?”

If they say they want other quotes first:

“Totally fair. What I’d recommend is getting Lex out first so you know what you actually need. Then you can compare the other quotes against a real recommendation instead of comparing random numbers. Is [Option A] or [Option B] better?”

2. “How much does it cost?”

“I understand wanting to know the investment. The reason we don’t give a price over the phone is that anyone giving you a firm number without testing the water and seeing the install conditions is guessing.”
“Lex will show you the actual results, give you the options, and review the exact price at the visit. If it makes sense, you can move forward. If it doesn’t, you don’t have to.”
“Would [Day/Time] or [Day/Time] work better?”

If they keep pressing:

“I don’t want to mislead you with a number that may not apply to your home. The visit is how you get the accurate answer.”

3. “I need to talk to my spouse.”

“Of course. That’s exactly why we want your spouse at the appointment.”
“It’s much easier for both of you to hear the results, ask questions, and look at the options together than for you to come alone and try to explain everything afterward.”
“What time works when both of you can be there, [Option A] or [Option B]?”

If spouse is unavailable:

“No problem. Let’s not waste Lex’s time or yours. What day can both of you attend within the next 72 hours?”

4. “We’ll do it later.”

“I understand. When you say later, do you mean you’re still gathering information, or this is not a priority right now?”

If still gathering information:

“That’s exactly what the visit is for. You don’t need to decide on a system today, but you can get the actual water results and understand your options.”

Then book:

“Let’s get the information handled while this is fresh. I have [Option A] or [Option B].”

If it is not a priority:

“Fair enough. What would need to change for it to become a priority?”

Listen.

“Got it. Based on that, it sounds like the right move is to [book within 72 hours / route to nurture]. Which is more accurate?”
Do not book fake appointments just to inflate the calendar.

5. “I need to think about it.”

“Absolutely. What specifically would you want to think about?”

If they cannot answer:

“Usually when someone says that before seeing the home, they’re missing information. Is it the water problem, the process, having both decision-makers there, or the investment?”

Handle the actual issue.

Then:

“That’s why the appointment exists. Lex can answer that properly after testing the water and seeing the home. You don’t have to commit to the system today, but you should get the facts.”
“Would [Option A] or [Option B] work better?”

No-Show Confirmation Process

Immediate confirmation text

“Hi [Name], this is [Setter] with Biscayne Water. You’re confirmed with Lex, our Senior Water Quality Specialist, for [Day] at [Time] at [Address]. He’ll test your water, inspect the install area, show you the results, and review your options. Please reply YES to confirm both you and [spouse] will be home.”

Night-before call or text

Send between 6 PM and 8 PM.

“Hi [Name], [Setter] with Biscayne Water. Quick confirmation for Lex tomorrow at [Time]. Are you and [spouse] still set to be home?”

If yes:

“Perfect. Please have access to the main shutoff and under the kitchen sink. Lex will text you when he’s on the way.”

If no reply, call once. If they still do not confirm, cancel the appointment and return it to Calendly.

Morning-of confirmation

“Good morning [Name], this is [Setter] with Biscayne Water. Lex is scheduled to meet you today at [Time]. Please reply YES so we know both decision-makers will be home and ready.”

If no response:

“We haven’t received confirmation, so we’re going to release today’s appointment rather than waste your time or Lex’s. Reply here and we’ll send you the next available times.”
No-show appointments are not sacred. Unconfirmed appointments get canceled and rebooked.

Lex on-the-way text

“Hi [Name], this is Lex with Biscayne Water. I’m heading your way now and should arrive around [Time]. I’ll see you and [spouse] shortly.”

Routing Rules

  • Renter: Tag Renter Path. Do not book Lex unless owner attends.
  • Well water: Tag Well-Water Path. Route to the appropriate well-water process.
  • No homeowner: Disqualify.
  • Only one decision-maker available: Do not book unless the other decision-maker confirms attendance.
  • Outside service area: Route to non-service-area response.
  • Refuses in-home evaluation: Do not quote price. Route to nurture or disqualify.
  • Asks for discount: No discount. Use legitimate bonuses only, approved by management.

Why This Works

  • It sells the visit, not the equipment. The homeowner commits to getting clarity, not buying blindly.
  • It preserves Lex’s calendar. Lex is positioned as the authority, not a generic salesperson. $100M Playbook Lead Nurture, page 22
  • It prevents commodity shopping. No firm phone quote without water testing and site conditions.
  • It forces both decision-makers into the room. This removes the spouse objection before Lex drives out.
  • It uses speed. Same-day, next-day, and day-after appointments outperform distant scheduling. $100M Playbook Lead Nurture, pages 20 and 24
  • It creates commitment before the appointment. The homeowner knows Lex will review options and collect payment if the solution fits.
  • It protects show rate. Automated reminders plus personal confirmation create volume and accountability. $100M Playbook Lead Nurture, pages 35 and 36
  • It eliminates “we’ll circle back.” Every appointment is booked live, and every next step is explicit. $100M Leads, page 183
