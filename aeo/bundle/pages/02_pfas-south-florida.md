**Short answer:** Yes. Miami-Dade's 2025 water report lists PFOS, a "forever chemical," at up to 33 parts per trillion and PFOA at up to 13. The federal limit for each is 4 ppt, enforced starting 2029. Boiling does not remove PFAS and a softener does not either. Reverse osmosis at the tap and certified PFAS media for the whole house do.

## What PFAS is, in plain words

PFAS are man-made chemicals used in non-stick pans, stain-proof fabric and firefighting foam. They break down very slowly, which is why people call them forever chemicals. In South Florida they reach the Biscayne aquifer, the shallow limestone layer most of our tap water is pumped from.

## How much is in South Florida tap water

| Utility (2025 report) | PFOS | PFOA | Federal limit (from 2029) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Miami-Dade WASD, Main System | up to 33 ppt | up to 13 ppt | 4 ppt each |
| Miami-Dade WASD, South Dade | up to 32 ppt | up to 13 ppt | 4 ppt each |

<small>Source: Miami-Dade Water and Sewer Department 2025 Water Quality Report. These are not violations today: the report says PFAS violations are not reported until 2029. In May 2026 the EPA proposed keeping the 4 ppt PFOA and PFOS limits and allowing utilities to request an extension to 2031; check epa.gov for current status.</small>

Researchers at Florida International University found 80 to 95 percent of tap samples from 18 Miami-Dade ZIP codes above the new 4 ppt limits. The highest levels showed up in Little Havana, Hialeah, Grapeland Heights near the airport, Homestead and Pembroke Pines.

<small>Source: Miami Herald, Aug 7, 2026, reporting FIU research by Natalia Soares Quinete and Carolina Cuchimaque Lugo (about 72 samples over five years).</small>

To be fair to the county: Miami-Dade says its water meets every current standard and is piloting PFAS treatment at its plants. That work is planned around the 2029 deadline. Your family drinks the water now.

## What removes PFAS, and what does not

| Method | Reduces PFAS? |
|---|---|
| Boiling | No. |
| Water softener (salt, ion exchange for hardness) | No, not on its own. |
| Fridge or pitcher filter | Some do, some do not. Check for an NSF/ANSI 53 or 58 PFOA/PFOS claim on that exact model. |
| Under-sink reverse osmosis | Yes. EWG lists reverse osmosis as a filtering option for PFAS in this utility. |
| Whole-home media certified for PFAS | Yes, when certified for the specific PFAS compounds. Covers showers and every tap, not only the kitchen. |

<small>Source: EWG Tap Water Database filtering options for Miami Dade Water and Sewer Authority. Certification claims apply only to the exact certified model.</small>

## PFAS questions we hear in Miami and Broward

**Does Miami tap water have PFAS?**
Yes. Miami-Dade's 2025 water report lists PFOS up to 33 ppt and PFOA up to 13 ppt. The federal limit is 4 ppt each, enforced from 2029.

**Does a water softener remove PFAS?**
No. A softener removes hardness minerals. PFAS needs reverse osmosis or media certified to reduce PFAS.

**Does reverse osmosis remove PFAS?**
Reverse osmosis is one of the most effective ways to reduce PFAS at a single tap. Check the exact model's NSF/ANSI 58 certification for PFOA and PFOS.

**Does boiling water remove forever chemicals?**
No. Boiling does not remove PFAS.

**Where is PFAS highest in South Florida?**
FIU researchers, as reported by the Miami Herald in August 2026, found the highest levels in Little Havana, Hialeah, Grapeland Heights near the airport, Homestead and Pembroke Pines.

**When does the PFAS limit take effect?**
The 4 ppt limits for PFOA and PFOS are enforced starting 2029. In May 2026 the EPA proposed letting utilities request an extension to 2031.
