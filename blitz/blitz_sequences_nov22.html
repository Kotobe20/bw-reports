1. TL;DR

What this is. A 7-day text + email follow-up for new leads. Grok and Viktor built it from the ACQ AI strategy Steve shared. Reps still call from the P1–P6 queues. These messages are the extra touches that used to depend on someone remembering to send them.

This PDF covers the 0–7 day blitz only. ACQ also described later lanes (no-show, presented but no sale, and long-term nurture). Those are not built yet.

How it starts. A new lead created on or after Nov 22, 2026, 4:27 PM ET is split by Lead Type:

  • Not Spanish → English sequence (Active now).
  • Spanish → Spanish sequence (still a draft, so it does not send).

Rep-typed leads stay out. Only leads from the intake zap or info@ can get in. The lead must still be in Potential status, less than 8 days old, and have no Housecall Pro customer ID.

How it stops. Before every message, Close checks the lead again. The sequence pauses, and nothing else sends, if any of these happen:

  • They text us (any inbound text).
  • A call lasts more than 2 minutes, either way.
  • A meeting is booked.
  • A Sold, Not Sold, Send Agreement, or Water Consult Completed card is logged.
  • Status leaves Potential.
  • They get a Housecall Pro customer ID.

A guard runs every 15 minutes from 8 AM–8 PM ET and pauses duplicates (same phone, or enrolled in both languages). A daily audit runs at 9 AM ET. Messages outside 9 AM–8 PM ET wait until the next window. Long waits can run about 15 minutes late. There are no call-task steps, so leads stay in the dial queues.

5. Quick notes for review

These are suggestions only. The live copy is unchanged. Scores use the Halbert + Ogilvy skill. ACQ notes are from the strategy Steve posted. Brand rules beat any of these ideas.

How the live English sequence scores today

Halbert: 7 / 10 · Ogilvy: 6 / 10

It already feels like a person, not an ad (Halbert, A-pile, BL Ch. 11). It is easy to read. Day 2 paints a real picture (dry skin, spots, bottled water). Day 4 gives two clear times, which matches ACQ’s BAMFAM ask. What it is missing: exact local facts (the city is never named), proof (no “1000+ customers, 5 stars,” no warranty), and a money line that stays inside house rules. Ogilvy would also want more facts and a proof point (HCATS #7, #12).

Spanish, as a shorter draft, scores a little lower: Halbert 6 / 10 · Ogilvy 6 / 10.

Rule flags

  • No system price is named. That rule is kept. The one soft risk is English Email 2, which says the call will show “what it costs.” That is not a price, but it is price talk in writing. See Suggestion 2.
  • No fake urgency, and no lifetime warranty. Day 7’s “close out your file” is a normal takeaway close, not a fake deadline.
  • Unused house lines (a miss, not a break): the copy never uses “$0 down, 0% interest, payments as low as $69/mo,” “1000+ customers, 5 stars,” or “extended 15 year warranty.”
  • The first text must stay true. It says “im pulling the water report for ur area now.” ACQ and Halbert both flag this: if no report is actually pulled, the line is bait (Halbert, BL Ch. 14). Confirm it is true, or change the wording.
  • Deliverability: English SMS sends from Amanda’s 786-823-3955, which has been rated red for call reputation. Her clean text-only number is 786-807-8273. Worth checking before real leads start getting these texts.

Suggestion 1 Name the city in the first text and first email

If adopted, about Halbert 8 / 10 · Ogilvy 8 / 10 on those two steps

ACQ wrote “your [City] water report.” Halbert says specifics and a customized line pull harder (BL Ch. 9–10, Ch. 23). Ogilvy says name the city (HCATS #25–31). “Ur area” is safe, but it is vague.

Only do this if the city (or ZIP) field is reliably filled. If it is often blank, keep “ur area.” Do not invent a city.
