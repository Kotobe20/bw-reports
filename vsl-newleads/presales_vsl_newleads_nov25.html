2. VSL analysis (Halbert + Ogilvy criteria)

Source: Doug's finished pre-sales VSL, BW_PreSalesZoom_FINAL_v5, 5 minutes 33 seconds, the file in the Drive folder he sent to the group this morning. Scores use the same Halbert + Ogilvy criteria already on file. I did not invent any claims. Every fact below is in the video.

Halbert 8 / 10 Ogilvy 7 / 10

Strong promise and proof, with a couple of craft misses. Nothing in the script should be stripped.

What it does well

  • Prospect is flagged immediately. The hook is a South Florida homeowner's real picture: shower glass that films over again, a shower that smells like a pool, expensive shampoo, kids, Publix bottled water, a fridge filter or a little shower filter that does not finish the job. (Halbert BL Ch. 16–17, desire as a word picture; Ogilvy HCATS #6, flag the prospect and localize.)
  • One clear promise. Know what is actually coming through every faucet, and use a system built around South Florida's water, not a generic two-tank setup. That is a benefit, not a slogan. (Ogilvy HCATS #2, #11.)
  • Interest is facts and a story, not adjectives. Visible problems (spots, chlorine smell) vs. what you cannot see (PFAS, arsenic, lead). Different areas face different challenges. A pretty set of tanks in the garage can still be uncertified for the contaminants you care about. The Bentonville, Arkansas contrast makes the "local company" reason why concrete. (Halbert BL Ch. 16, 21; Ogilvy HCATS #7.)
  • Specific proof, already in Doug's mouth. NSF 53 certified PFAS stage; manufacturer testing up to 99.82% reduction of five PFAS compounds; one of those types of PFAS found at over 2,500 times the health scientists' recommendation in Miami's water supply; dedicated stages (catalytic carbon, softener, metals guard, PFAS guard, seven-stage RO with remineralization). (Halbert BL Ch. 23, exact details; Ogilvy HCATS #7, #12.)
  • Honest close and risk reversal. The team will reach out, ask about the home, and only then set an in-home visit if it is the right step. "You don't have to buy a system just to understand your options." "You don't have to figure this out all by yourself." Pick up when we call, or use the link to schedule. (Halbert BL Ch. 16, close; checklist item 9.)

What I would not change in the video

  • I would not cut or rewrite any of Doug's lines. A couple of spoken slips are already cleaned in the captions ("chloramine and chlorine," "2,500 times the health scientists' recommendation," "for homes where heavy metals are a concern"). Leave the captions as they are.
  • The mid-video "I'm Doug, one of the founders of Biscayne Water" is a real miss against Ogilvy's "brand in the first 3 seconds" video rule (HCATS #24). It is not a reason to recut this version before we start sending it. If Doug ever does a new take, put his name and Biscayne Water in the opening.

What has to be true around the video

  • The link has to be a familiar YouTube player, unlisted. That is Doug's idea, and it is the right one. A YouTube "unpublished" or draft file cannot be watched by a lead. Unlisted can.
  • The player needs a button under it. The last part of the VSL tells people to click the button just below the video, or use the link below to schedule a visit. A plain YouTube URL has no such button. The honest setup is an unlisted YouTube embed on a simple Biscayne Water page, with the schedule button directly under the player, and the same link in the YouTube description as backup.
