TL;DR

  • There is not one single YouTube video that lists every A1 Garage tool and also describes a recorder clipped to a technician’s uniform. The clearest “here is the software I use” list is Ken Baden’s Kitchen Table interview (Jul 8, 2024). Later videos add Power BI, Samsara, and FieldSpark.
  • The tool people are thinking of is Rilla Voice (now branded Rilla, at rilla.com). Tommy’s repeated description: the tech puts a phone or tablet out during the appointment. AI transcribes the visit and grades script, pace, tone, talk time, open-ended questions, the company story, financing, and service agreements.
  • I could not find Tommy saying techs attach a device to their uniforms in the transcripts I pulled. That detail is likely a mix-up with “hands-free” marketing, or with a competitor (Siro), which also records from a phone or tablet app, not a badge mic.
  • By October 2026, Tommy says A1 built its own version, FieldSpark, and names Siro and Rilla as the other tools in that category. He has said Rilla and Siro cost more than ServiceTitan at A1 Garage’s scale. FieldSpark has no public price.
  • Fit for Biscayne Water: the category is a strong match for in-home water consults, but Florida is a two-party consent state. Someone has to tell the homeowner and get a yes before recording. The software only pays off if a manager actually coaches from the recordings.

3. Deep dive: the in-person recording and grading tool

What people remembered vs. what Tommy actually said

The group asked about a device techs attach to their uniforms that records the appointment and grades cadence, pacing, script, and objections. In the videos I could transcribe, Tommy never describes a clip-on uniform recorder. He describes a phone or iPad placed out during the visit. The product he named for years is Rilla Voice. In 2026 he says A1 Garage also built FieldSpark for the same job, and he names Siro as another vendor in the category. Siro’s public site also describes a phone or tablet app, not a badge microphone.

Rilla Voice: how it works

Rilla’s own site (rilla.com, fetched Nov 24, 2026) puts it in three steps: the rep talks to the customer in person, records the conversation in the Rilla app (iPhone or Android; managers use a web login), and the manager coaches through “virtual ride-alongs.” Setup is advertised as 1 hour 45 minutes. It integrates with ServiceTitan; Tommy has also said it works with Housecall Pro and Service Fusion.

“Rilla Voice is a AI tool that if you put your cell phone or tablet out… it allows you to record the conversation and it picks up. Tonality it picks up. Did you ask an open ended question? Did you tell the company story? Did you follow the sales specifications of your business? … it tells you the words per minute you’re talking and who talked more… They show the price or the investment in monthly payments, instead of giving the total price… They slow down when they give the price. Rilla Voice tells us all that data.” Tommy Mello, Owned & Operated “LEGENDS” interview (written transcript; also consistent with the Kitchen Table and Potomac interviews)

What it grades, based on Tommy and on Rilla’s public pages:

  • Script / process adherence (A1 Garage uses an eight-step sales process; Rilla’s public UI shows steps such as greeting, inspection, presentation, pricing, and financing)
  • Talk ratio: who talked more. Tommy said A1’s top 10% of techs talk far less than the customer
  • Words per minute and whether the tech matched the customer’s pace and tone
  • Open-ended questions
  • Whether the company story was told, and whether it was told after the price
  • Whether price was presented as a monthly payment, and whether the tech slowed down at the price
  • Whether financing and service agreements were offered (a pre-Rilla pain point Tommy has described)
  • Filler words (“um,” “you know”), in the Potomac interview
  • Keyword search (for example, “did they offer a service agreement?”)

Managers get scorecards and highlight reels of top performers. Techs can listen to themselves. Tommy’s implementation point, repeated often: the software does nothing if techs are not required to record, and if nobody is coaching. He has said it should be a specialist’s full-time job, not an extra task for an area manager.

On results: Rilla’s marketing and case pages credit A1 with a large jump in average ticket (Rilla has used “almost double” / “2X”). Those are vendor claims. I did not independently audit A1 Garage’s books. Tommy’s own ROI framing is simpler: if one extra job or better option presentation covers the bill, it pays for itself, and “Rilla Voice is not cheap.”

Consent: Rilla’s FAQ (fetched Nov 24, 2026) lists 11 states that require the customer to be told first, including Florida, California, Massachusetts, Connecticut, Maryland, Illinois, Washington, Montana, Pennsylvania, New Hampshire, and Oregon. Tommy has used “12 states” and has named Florida, New York, and California. State lists change; this is not legal advice. Rilla’s terms put the duty to get consent on the company using the tool, not on Rilla.

FieldSpark: A1 Garage’s later tool

fieldspark.ai says Tommy built it because existing tools were not fast enough, were not affordable at A1 Garage’s scale, and did not focus enough on the communication skills that move the needle. The public site lists AI role-play against lifelike “customers,” tonality and pace training, scorecards, script comparison, sentiment, and “hands-free call capture” on a mobile phone or tablet, plus a ServiceTitan integration (other CRMs “in the works,” per a FieldSpark employee interview on YouTube).

Tommy’s own reason, Jun 19, 2026: Rilla and Siro’s price point is “more expensive than ServiceTitan,” and most companies that buy those tools do not staff a management team around the recordings. FieldSpark’s site says the first mission was to fuel A1 Garage, and that other companies using it is “a bonus.” There is no public price. Email info@fieldspark.ai.

One-line fit for Biscayne Water. This category is a strong match for in-home water treatment sales (script, options, objections, financing), but Florida requires consent before recording, and the buy only makes sense if a manager will actually listen and coach; start with quotes from Rilla and FieldSpark, and confirm Housecall Pro / Close support, before signing an annual seat contract.

4. Practical notes if we ever trial this

  • Ask the homeowner first. Florida is a two-party consent state. Build a simple doorstep / intro line and an opt-out path. About 5% of customers asked a different company (on a Rilla panel video) to stop; they stopped the recording and tagged the account.
  • Techs will resist at first. Tommy and other owners say the same thing: people hate being recorded until they hear themselves and see tickets go up. Rollout is a culture problem, not just a software install.
  • Do not buy it as a babysitter. Tommy’s warning: if nobody coaches, you paid for a library of files. Budget a person, not just licenses.
  • CRM fit is unconfirmed for our stack until a demo. Biscayne Water runs Housecall Pro and Close, not ServiceTitan. Tommy has said Rilla works with Housecall Pro. FieldSpark publicly highlights ServiceTitan and says other CRMs are in progress. Confirm this on a call.
  • Price is the open item. Rilla is quote-only and, at third-party estimates, is a five-figure annual commitment even for a small crew. FieldSpark claims to be cheaper at scale but also has no public price. Get both in writing, for our actual headcount, before anyone gets excited about “what Tommy uses.”
